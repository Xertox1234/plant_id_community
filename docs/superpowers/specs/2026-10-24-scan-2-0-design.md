# Scan 2.0: guided camera, diagnosis first — design

Date: 2026-10-24 · Status: draft for owner review · Platform: Flutter mobile app (primary), Django backend

## Intent

The scan is the flagship feature, and today it is a form: two buttons that hand
off to the phone's own camera or photo picker, a preview, an "Identify Plant"
button, and a blank wait. The goal of 2.0 is a camera experience that feels
made for plants. It coaches you to a good shot, works in the background, and
answers the question the app exists for: **what is wrong with my plant?**
Identification supports the diagnosis, and the diagnosis leads to treatment
and follow-up.

Marking in this document: **[owner]** means the owner decided it in the
brainstorm. **[call]** means the author decided it and the owner has not
confirmed it. Unmarked text is description or context.

## Current state (verified in code, 2026-10-24)

- `plant_community_mobile/lib/features/camera/camera_screen.dart` uses
  `image_picker` (the system camera or gallery). It has no viewfinder, no
  guidance and no animation. Upload progress is tracked but never shown
  (`_uploadProgress`, marked unused).
- **Live bug.** `firebase_storage_service.dart:69` uploads to
  `plant_images/<uuid><ext>`. `firebase/storage.rules` only allows
  `plant-identifications/{userId}/{imageId}` and denies every other path.
  Every identify fails with "user is not authorized". The owner reproduced the
  error, and the path mismatch is the likely cause. The deployed rules have not
  been checked against the repo copy.
- `plant_identification_service.dart` uploads the photo twice: once to Firebase
  Storage, and again as multipart to `POST /plant-identification/identify/`.
  The service keeps only the name, scientific name, description and care list.
  The response also carries `confidence`, `suggestions` (alternatives),
  `disease_detection`, `disease_detection_status` and
  `disease_detection_reason` (`backend/apps/plant_identification/api/simple_views.py`),
  and the app drops all of them.
- Identify runs **synchronously** on the server (todo 461). A Celery worker is
  live in prod.
- `PlantNetService` already accepts several images with organ tags. The combined
  service always sends one image labelled `leaf`
  (`combined_identification_service.py:408`).
- Diagnosis today is a separate screen (`features/diagnose/`): one photo, a
  required symptoms text, an optional condition and location. It calls
  `POST /plant-identification/disease-requests/` (synchronous, signed in). The
  identify call separately runs Plant.id's health assessment.
- Existing backend fields include `is_accepted` on identification results,
  `ai_care_instructions` and `care_instructions_generated_at` on results, and a
  user-saved care instructions model (`saved_care_instructions`).
- The app's **My Collection** screen (`features/collection/`) is backed by
  Firestore. The backend also has `UserPlantCollection`/`UserPlant`, and the
  garden calendar has its own `Plant` list. These are three separate systems.
- Push (`firebase_messaging`, live in prod) and `flutter_local_notifications`
  are both installed. The garden calendar has recurring `CareTask`s and a
  server-side reminder sweep. The app does not schedule reminders on the phone.

## Goals

1. An in-app, full-screen live camera with guided capture. **[owner]**
2. Diagnosis-first capture: the first shot is the problem. **[owner]**
3. A **Just identify it** mode for healthy plants. **[owner]**
4. Processing happens in the background. The scan screen closes, you keep using
   the app, and a notification arrives when the result is ready. **[owner]**
5. A results screen with identification and diagnosis, **both with confidence
   scores**. **[owner]**
6. Accept or reject the identification based on its confidence. **[owner]**
7. A general care guide and, when a diagnosis is found, a custom treatment plan.
   Each is **generated only when the user taps for it** and saved to the
   profile. Generated guides are cached. **[owner]**
8. Add to collection. **[owner]**
9. Reminders after a diagnosis. **[owner]**

## Non-goals (this round)

- Rewriting the web scan/diagnose pages. Mobile is primary. **[call]**
- On-device ML species recognition. Guidance uses cheap frame heuristics (see
  below). **[call]**
- Merging the three plant-list systems. 2.0 uses My Collection (Firestore) and
  does not touch the others. **[call]**

## Anti-goals (would make this a failure even if it works)

The owner did not name any. These are the author's, inferred from the conversation. **[call]**

- Capture feels slower or more demanding than today. Only the first shot is
  required, and every later step has an obvious Skip.
- A low-confidence guess presented as fact.
- AI spend on guides nobody asked for.
- A scan lost because the app was put in the background.

## The flow

### 1. Capture: the field guide

**Decided by the owner**

- Full-screen in-app camera. Opening Scan never shows the system camera.
- Guided steps with a chip row (the current step is highlighted, and a chip can
  be tapped to jump). A tray of finished shots fills as you go. **Done** sends.
- **Diagnosis mode (default)**, in step order:
  1. **"Show me what's wrong"**: a close-up of the symptom. **Required.**
  2. **Leaf**: identifies the species. Skippable.
  3. **Whole plant**: shape, plus problems across the whole plant. Skippable.
  4. **Flower**, if it has one. Skippable.
- **Every step after the first has its own visible Skip button next to the
  shutter.** **[owner]** Done works any time after one shot.
- **Just identify it** chip: switches to leaf-first identify mode (leaf
  required, then whole plant and flower, all skippable). **[owner]**
- Live hints while aiming ("Point at the problem", "Move closer", "Too dark,
  tap ⚡", "Hold steady"). The frame snaps green, with a haptic tick, when the
  shot is acceptable. **[owner, from the chosen mockup]**

**Author's calls**

- Hints come from on-device frame heuristics: brightness, blur (Laplacian
  variance or similar), green-pixel coverage for "is a plant in frame". It does
  not truly detect leaves. **[call]**
- The user always taps the shutter. No auto-capture. **[call]**
- Controls: flash or torch, tap to focus, pinch to zoom, a gallery button (a
  gallery photo fills the current step), close. **[call]**
- The problem step has the strictest blur and closeness thresholds. **[call]**
- Only the problem step is required. **[call]**

### 2. Submit and background processing

**Decided by the owner:** after Done, the scan screen closes, the user keeps
using the app, and a notification arrives on completion. Tapping it opens the
results screen.

**Author's calls**

- The photos upload while the app is in the foreground (seconds, with progress
  shown in a small persistent "Checking your plant…" pill). The **server** then
  processes as a Celery job and sends an FCM push when done. On-phone background
  processing is rejected, because iOS suspends backgrounded apps mid-job. **[call]**
- The pill stays visible across the app until the result arrives. Tapping it
  while it is still pending shows the status. **[call]**
- If the app is in the foreground when the job finishes, show an in-app banner
  instead of (or as well as) the system push. Use the existing
  `push_foreground_presenter`. **[call]**
- The identify endpoint must accept several images with an organ/role tag per
  image (`problem`, `leaf`, `whole`, `flower`), and pass them to PlantNet as
  organs and to Plant.id as several images. **[call]** The mapping of the
  `problem` role to provider organs is left to the builder.
- Scan results must be stored server-side so the notification can deep-link to
  them by id. **[call]**

### 3. Results screen

**Decided by the owner**

- Shows the identification and, when applicable, the diagnosis, **each with a
  confidence score**.
- Accept or reject the identification ("That's right" / "Not my plant").
- An optional **care guide**, generated only on tap and saved to the profile.
- When diagnosed, an optional **custom treatment plan**, generated only on tap
  and saved to the profile.
- **Add to collection.**

**Author's calls** (see mockup `results-screen.html` in the brainstorm session)

- Order: the diagnosis card first (because diagnosis is the main question),
  then identification, then the care guide. A healthy result shows a "Looks
  healthy" line instead of the diagnosis card. **[call]**
- Confidence shows as a percentage plus a colour bar. Bands: ≥70% green,
  40–69% amber, <40% red. These are placeholder thresholds to tune. **[call]**
- Below 70%, the identification card shows the top alternatives from
  `suggestions` ("Is it one of these?"), lets the user pick one, and offers to
  add a missing shot (flower, whole plant). Choosing an alternative counts as
  accepting it. **[call]**
- "Not my plant" opens the alternatives, plus "None of these", which leads to
  rescan. **[call]**
- The care guide is locked until the identification is accepted, so no guide is
  generated for the wrong species. The treatment plan is not locked, because a
  symptom can be treated before the species is confirmed. **[call]**
- The typed symptoms field becomes an optional note on the results screen. **[call]**

### 4. Care guides and treatment plans

**Decided by the owner**

- Guides are separate items in the profile (working name **My Guides**), not
  part of the collection entry.
- A collection card **links to** a saved guide for that plant, when one exists.

**Author's calls**

- The general care guide is cached **per species** and shared across users. The
  treatment plan is cached **per (species, diagnosis)** pair. Each user's saved
  copy is a separate record that points at the cached content. **[call]**
- Guides are generated server-side (the AI key stays on the server), and the
  user-saved model lives in the backend. Reuse or extend the existing
  `ai_care_instructions` / saved care instructions models. **[call]**
- If a guide is saved after the plant is added to the collection (or the other
  way round), the link appears on its own. Match guides to collection plants by
  the scan/identification id they came from. **[call]**

### 5. Add to collection

**Decided by the owner:** it saves to the app's **My Collection** screen
(Firestore). The entry is the user's photo of the plant, plus its name and the
accepted identification.

**Author's calls**

- The Firebase Storage upload moves from "every scan" to **only on Add to
  collection**. It uploads to a rules-permitted, user-scoped path
  (`plant-identifications/{uid}/…`). This also fixes the live
  "unauthorized" bug. **[call]**
- The collection photo is the best non-problem shot (whole plant, then leaf,
  then problem). **[call]**

### 6. Reminders after a diagnosis

**Decided by the owner:** after a diagnosis, the user can set timers or
reminders.

**Author's calls**

- Each treatment-plan step has a bell button, and the plan has one **Set all
  reminders** button. The user can edit the time and repeat before saving. **[call]**
- The last reminder in every plan is a **follow-up scan** ("Check on your
  Monstera — 14 days"). It opens the camera in diagnosis mode with the original
  problem photo shown faintly over the viewfinder, to line up the same angle.
  The result compares before and after and says better, same or worse. **[call]**
- Reminders are tied to the treatment plan, not to a collection plant, so they
  work for unsaved plants. They are delivered by server push, reusing the
  garden-calendar recurring-task and reminder-sweep machinery where it fits, or
  a parallel model if `CareTask`'s tie to the garden `Plant` doesn't fit. **[call]**

## Settled after the builder check

- **Care guides and treatment plans are premium-only.** **[owner]** They follow
  the existing premium AI pattern: `IsPremiumUser`, a per-user throttle, a
  feature flag and a forum-style `*_BUDGET_LIMIT`. Free users still get the
  full capture, background processing, results with confidence scores,
  accept/reject and Add to collection. On the results screen the guide buttons
  stay visible to free users and open an upgrade prompt. **[call]** Reminders
  hang off the treatment plan, so they are premium too, and so is the
  follow-up scan they lead to. **[call]**
- **Treatment plans are shared, not personal.** **[owner]** A plan is
  generated once per (species, diagnosis) and served from the cache to every
  later premium user with the same pair. The user's note and photos do **not**
  go into the prompt. If the species is not yet accepted, the key uses the top
  identification guess. If the user later rejects that species, their saved
  plan is marked "made for [species]" and the screen offers the plan for the
  corrected species, generated or served from the cache. **[call]**

- **Sign-in is required to scan.** Prod already requires it
  (`IsAuthenticatedForIdentification`; anonymous identification only works
  under `DEBUG`), and jobs, push, guides, collection and reminders all need an
  identity. A signed-out user who taps Scan gets the sign-in screen first, then
  the camera. **[call]**
- **Where scan photos live.** Each scan's photos are stored server-side with the
  scan job, under the same retention as the job record, and are deleted with
  the user's account. When a treatment plan's reminders are set, the problem
  photo is kept for as long as the plan exists, so the follow-up overlay and
  before/after view work for plants that were never saved. The builder picks
  the store (R2 via `USE_R2`, or a user-scoped Firebase path). **[call]**
- **The follow-up verdict needs no new AI.** The follow-up scan re-runs
  diagnosis on the new photo. Better / same / worse comes from comparing the
  two results: is the diagnosis the same, how did its confidence and severity
  move, or is it now healthy. The two photos are shown side by side. A
  vision-model comparison is out of scope for 2.0. **[call]**
- **The old Diagnose screen and endpoints.** The multi-image async flow is a
  **new** endpoint. `identify/` and `disease-requests/` stay working and
  unchanged for installed app versions. In the 2.0 app, every Diagnose entry
  point opens the new scan in diagnosis mode, and the old screen is removed
  from navigation once the new flow ships. **[call]**

## Constraints

- Mobile follows Canopy (`plant_community_mobile/docs/patterns/flutter-ui-design.md`)
  and Riverpod 3.x.
- AI generation is budgeted and server-side (the project's existing pattern
  of per-feature budget limits and throttles applies).
- The repo is public. No credentials in the client.
- Treat iOS and Android camera permission denial as a first-class state.

## Left to the builder

- The camera package (`camera` or an alternative) and the heuristic thresholds.
- Motion details: the bracket snap, chip transitions, tray animation, the
  results reveal.
- The scan-job API shape (create, status, result), its retention, and failure
  and retry behaviour.
- How the `problem` role maps onto PlantNet organs and the Plant.id health
  assessment. Whether the existing `disease-requests` path or the identify
  health assessment produces the diagnosis.
- The design of the My Guides screen.
- Phasing. A suggested order: (0) the storage-path fix as a standalone PR, so
  scan works today; (1) the in-app guided camera; (2) multi-image and async
  jobs with push; (3) the results screen; (4) on-demand guides with caching and
  My Guides; (5) reminders and follow-up scan.
